Problem statement
In the Egyptian marriage market, the things that actually end a relationship — religion, whether you want children and when, relocating cities, how close you live to family, money — are exactly the things dating apps surface last. People sort by photos, invest months, then discover an incompatibility that was knowable on day one. The traditional خاطبة (matchmaker) screened for precisely those constraints up front, but she didn't scale, and she never showed her reasoning.
Solution overview
Khatba inverts the usual order. A 90-second, 8-question assessment captures the hard constraints, and the engine eliminates first — striking out anyone who fails a dealbreaker and naming which one. Only the survivors get scored, across six weighted pillars. Every score comes with a plain-Arabic reason for both the agreements and the gaps, and the user can re-weight the pillars or switch any dealbreaker off to see the ranking rebuild live.
The creative twist
The product sells itself by showing you what it refuses to show you. The “لحظة الفرق” (moment of difference) screen animates candidates being struck off one by one with the reason beside each, lands on “فضل ١٢ من ١٩” — 12 left out of 19 — then puts a normal dating app side by side with it: it would have shown you 19 profiles sorted by photos, and the real differences would surface months later. Turning exclusion into the headline feature, rather than hiding it, is a genuinely counter-intuitive product decision.

How the matching actually works

Two stages: hard filters, then weighted scoring. Both are fully exposed to the user.

Stage 1 — Dealbreakers الموانع

Binary. Fail one and you're out — with the reason shown. Each can be toggled off to test the effect.
✕اختلاف الديانة— religion mismatch
✕السن بره النطاق— age outside range
✕المدينة والانتقال— city / willingness to relocate
✕الحالة الاجتماعية— marital status
✕وجود أولاد— existing children
✕موقف متضارب من الإنجاب— conflicting stance on having children
✕مستوى التديّن أقل من الحد— religiosity below threshold

Stage 2 — Six pillars الأركان الستة

Scored and weighted. The user sets each multiplier themselves and the ranking recomputes live.
1التديّن— religiosity & practice
2الحياة العملية— work & career rhythm
3البيت والمال— housing & finances
4الأسرة— family closeness & involvement
5الأولاد— children: whether, when, how many
6الشخصية ونمط الحياة— personality & lifestyle

Tech stack & the hard problem solved
The hard part wasn't computing a score — it was making the score defensible. Most matching products emit an opaque number. Khatba had to generate, for every candidate pair and every pillar, a specific human-readable sentence covering both agreement and gap: “متقاربين ماديًا: هيأجّر وهيأجّر” (financially aligned — both renting), “فجوة مالية: هيأجّر مقابل الأهل هيساعدوا” (financial gap — he'll rent, her family will help), “التوقيت مختلف: بعد سنة لسنتين مقابل مش متأكد” (timing differs — 1–2 years vs. unsure).
It also handles the genuinely ambiguous case rather than forcing a verdict — where two people are neither aligned nor opposed, it says so: “مفيش اتفاق قوي ولا اختلاف حاد — كله في المنطقة الوسط.” On top of that, the whole product is built right-to-left in Egyptian colloquial rather than translated Modern Standard Arabic, which is what makes it read like a person and not a form.
Worth noting for judging: the flow doesn't stop at a match list. It continues through طلب التعارف (introduction request) → المحادثة (chat) → التحضير (preparation) → اللقاء (the meeting), with 25 fully-written personas behind it. This is a complete product journey, not a single screen.
